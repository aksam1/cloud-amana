# Chapter 6 — Prophet Ibrahim (إبراهيم)

The chapter after Salih, and the whole of Ibrahim's story as the Quran and the authentic hadith tell it:
the idols of his people and his search through the star, the moon and the sun; the broken idols, the fire
made cool, the king who argued and the four birds; the journey west; Hajar and the infant Ismail left in
the valley, and the spring; the dream and the ransom; the honoured guests, the news of Ishaq and the
angels going on toward the cities of Lut (Chapter 7); the House raised with Ismail, the call to
pilgrimage, and the friend of God. Ismail's and Ishaq's own lives as prophets are left for Chapter 8. Scenes are in `src/story/chapters/c6/`; image prompts
follow the shared Troy + Three Kingdoms style (`npm run prompts` adds the style and the content rules).

**Content rules kept throughout.** God is never depicted, in any form or light: the fire turns cool on
screen, and His words appear only as the verse. Ibrahim, Lut and the angels appear only as light, far away
or from behind; Sarah, Azar, the king and the people as dim figures of light; no faces. Idols are carved
stone and wood: they may be shown, and broken, but never honoured by the camera. Nothing violent is shown:
Ibrahim goes into the fire and is not harmed. No music.

**New in the engine for this chapter.**
- *A living sky* (`heavens`): a time-lapse sky whose colours run through night, dawn, day and dusk on the
  beat's own clock, with a star, the moon and the sun rising and setting along real arcs behind the hills,
  and the whole scene's light and haze following the time of day.
- *Shattering cards* (`shatter`): a painted idol breaks into shards that tumble and fall.
- *A shader fire* (`fire`): animated ink-wash flames with dark ink at their edges, sparks and heat, which
  can turn cool (`cool`), silver-blue and calm.
- *Birds of light* (`light` `kind: "bird"`): wings beating in flight along 3D paths, folded once landed.
- *Walls raised course by course* (`masonry`): every stone its own tone and inked joint, the newest pale;
  robed in black and gold for the House as it stands today.
- *A welling spring* (`spring`): a pool spreading in rings from where the water rises, a bank of sand
  gathered round it.
- *Circling crowds* (`crowd` `orbit`): pilgrims going round the House, passing behind it and in front.
- Figures that bow in prostration and an infant lying swaddled; whole days and nights run past in a beat
  (`tod` days); a ram drawn only as light.

## Scenes

### 60 · The City of Idols · مدينة الأصنام (`ur`)
- **Ayah:** 6:74. **Engine:** the ziggurat painter; a crowd streaming to the temple.
- **Beats.** (1) Dawn over a river city of mud brick, a stepped temple tower rising above it, crowds going
  up its ramps. *Say:* Long after Thamud, in a city between great rivers, people bowed to idols of stone
  and wood. (2) Inside a workshop: rows of carved figures, a dim figure at work. *Say:* Ibrahim's own father,
  Azar, carved them with his hands. (3) A young steady light stands among the idols, still. *Say:* But
  Ibrahim, his son, could not bow to what hands had made. (4) **Verse 6:74.**
- **Sound:** water, city → work → air. **Art:** the city and its temple tower are imagined after the
  ziggurats of Mesopotamia; the people appear as dim lights.
- **Image prompt:** Dawn over a city of sun-baked mud brick between two broad rivers, palm groves along
  the banks, a vast stepped temple tower rising from its heart with long ramps where streams of dim amber
  lights climb toward the summit; inside a workshop, rows of carved stone and wooden figures with tall
  headdresses and blank faces stand in ranks, a dim figure bent at work among them, and a single young
  steady white light standing apart; dust in slanting light; ochre, brick red, river blue and gold;
  ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 61 · The Star, the Moon and the Sun · النجم والقمر والشمس (`search`)
- **Ayah:** 6:79. **Engine:** the living sky, end to end.
- **Beats.** (1) Night falls over the plain; a bright star rises; Ibrahim's light on a hill, from behind.
  *Say:* When night covered him, he saw a star rise, and said: is this my lord? But it set, and he said: I
  do not love things that set. (2) The moon rises, full and bright, crosses, and sinks. *Say:* Then he saw the
  moon rising, and it too went down. (3) Dawn: the sun rises, the land floods with gold, the sun crosses
  and sets. *Say:* Then the sun, the greatest of them, and it set as well. (4) **Verse 6:79**, at dusk.
- **Sound:** crickets, air → wind → birds. **Art:** the hill and the plain are imagined.
- **Image prompt:** A lone steady white light seen from behind on a rocky hill above a dark plain; the
  night sky deep indigo with countless stars, one bright star rising and setting; then a great full moon
  rising silver over the hills and sinking again; then dawn flooding the plain with gold as the sun climbs,
  crosses the sky and sets in crimson; the whole sky changing in one continuous time-lapse; indigo, silver,
  gold and crimson; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 62 · My Father · حوار مع الأب (`father`)
- **Ayah:** 19:47. **Engine:** two lights in a lamplit room; a door of daylight.
- **Beats.** (1) Lamplit workshop: Ibrahim's light before his father's dim figure. *Say:* Gently, Ibrahim
  asked his father why he worshipped what could neither hear nor see. (2) The father turns away, angry.
  *Say:* His father turned on him in anger and told him to go away. (3) Ibrahim's light walks out into the
  daylight. **Verse 19:47.**
- **Sound:** fire (small lamp), voice → air. **Art:** the scene is imagined from 19:42–47.
- **Image prompt:** A dim workshop of carved idols lit by a single oil lamp; a steady white light standing
  before a dim amber figure seated among the idols; the amber figure turning away; then the white light
  walking out through a doorway into bright daylight; warm lamp amber, deep umber shadows, a slice of white
  sun; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 63 · The Broken Idols · تحطيم الأصنام (`broken`)
- **Ayah:** 21:58. **Engine:** shattering cards.
- **Beats.** (1) The people stream out of the city to their festival. *Say:* On the day of their festival
  the people left the city, and Ibrahim stayed behind. (2) The temple hall, empty, idols in rows. *Say:* He
  went into the temple, where the idols stood in silence. (3) The idols break apart, one after another,
  all but the largest. *Say:* He broke them to pieces, all but the largest of them. (4) **Verse 21:58.**
- **Sound:** crowd, voices → echo → stone, drop → air. **Art:** the axe left on the largest idol is from
  the commentaries and is not shown.
- **Image prompt:** A vast temple hall of mud brick and cedar beams, shafts of light falling through high
  openings onto rows of carved idols with tall headdresses and blank faces; the idols cracking and breaking
  into tumbling shards of stone and painted wood, dust rising in the light beams, only the largest idol left
  standing at the end of the hall; ochre, faded lapis and gilt, stone grey; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 64 · Ask Them · المواجهة (`ask`)
- **Ayah:** 21:63. **Engine:** jeers swirling, then falling silent.
- **Beats.** (1) The crowd returns and finds the broken idols. *Say:* When they returned and saw what had
  been done, they brought Ibrahim before everyone. (2) Jagged strokes of anger swirl around his light.
  *Say:* Was it you who did this to our gods, they asked. (3) The strokes fall still; the crowd lowers.
  **Verse 21:63.**
- **Sound:** crowd, jeer → jeer → air. **Art:** the crowd's anger is jagged strokes, never words.
- **Image prompt:** The temple court crowded with figures of dim amber light around a single steady white
  light, jagged dark-red strokes of anger swirling around it; behind, the broken idols lie in shards before
  the one great idol still standing; then the strokes falling away and the crowd's lights lowering in
  silence; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 65 · The Fire · النار (`fire`)
- **Ayah:** 21:69. **Engine:** the shader fire and its cool transform.
- **Beats.** (1) An immense fire roars in a walled pit, smoke and sparks rising. *Say:* They built a fire
  so great that its heat kept them far away, and cast Ibrahim into it. (2) His light goes down into the
  flames. *Say:* But the fire did not burn him. (3) The flames turn silver and cool; light settles. **Verse
  21:69.**
- **Sound:** roar, fire → fire → air, spring. **Art:** how he was cast in is from the commentaries and is
  not shown; the fire's turning cool is drawn from the verse; nothing violent is shown.
- **Image prompt:** A colossal fire in a walled pit at night, flames like ink-wash tongues of orange and
  crimson leaping higher than the walls, sparks streaming into a black sky, a vast crowd of dim lights held
  far back by the heat; a single steady white light descending into the heart of the flames; then the fire
  turning silver and cool, its tongues slowing into calm pale light, the white light resting at its centre
  unharmed; ember red turning to silver-blue; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 66 · The King Who Argued · الملك المجادل (`king`)
- **Ayah:** 2:258 (the part with the sun). **Engine:** the living sky at dawn.
- **Beats.** (1) A throne hall of brick and lapis; a dark figure on the throne. *Say:* A king claimed that
  he, too, gave life and death. (2) Through the hall's great doorway, the sun rising in the east. *Say:*
  Ibrahim answered: my Lord brings the sun up from the east; bring it up from the west. (3) **Verse 2:258**,
  the king silent.
- **Sound:** echo, voice → air → air. **Art:** the king is traditionally named Nimrod; he appears only as a
  dark figure, and the hall is imagined.
- **Image prompt:** A towering throne hall of mud brick and glazed lapis tiles, ranks of columns, a dark
  figure seated high on a stepped throne; a small steady white light standing far below before it; through
  the great doorway behind, the sun rising red-gold in the east and pouring a long beam of light across the
  floor; lapis blue, gold, deep shadow; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 67 · The Four Birds · الطيور الأربعة (`birds`)
- **Ayah:** 2:260 (the part with his request). **Engine:** birds of light flying to four summits and back.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* Ibrahim once asked God to show him how He gives life to the dead: not from doubt, he said, but so that his heart might be at rest. (2) *Say:* He was told to take four birds and keep them near him. (3) *Say:* Then to set a part of them on each of the hills around him, and to call them. (4) **Verse 2:260** (Ibrahim's request to be shown how the dead are given life, to his heart being at rest), placed sky.
- **Sound:** wind, air → birds, air → wind → wind, birds. **Art:** The birds appear only as light. What was done to them is told, not shown: on screen they fly to the hills and come back. Ibrahim appears as a steady light, far away. The hills are imagined.
- **Image prompt:** A lone steady white light on a grassy hilltop at dusk, an arm raised to a violet and rose sky; four birds made of white light circling down to him; then the four birds flying out to four distant summits around him and resting there as faint glows; then all four streaking back to him across the evening sky; dusk violet, rose, pale gold; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 68 · The Journey · الهجرة (`journey`)
- **Ayah:** 37:99. **Engine:** a long montage of lands in one flight.
- **Beats.** (1) Three lights leave the river city at dawn. *Say:* Ibrahim left his people, and with him
  went his wife Sarah and Lut, who believed in him. (2) Across steppe, river crossings and mountains.
  *Say:* They travelled far toward the west, trusting God to guide them. (3) Green hills of a new land at
  evening. **Verse 37:99.**
- **Sound:** water → wind, desert → birds. **Art:** the route is imagined; Sarah and Lut appear as lights.
- **Image prompt:** A short line of three steady lights leaving a walled river city at dawn; then crossing
  a vast grassy steppe, fording a broad river, climbing a pass between blue mountains, the lights tiny in
  immense landscapes; then green hills with olive trees under a golden evening sky; ink-wash and parchment
  texture.

### 69 · A Forbearing Son · ولادة إسماعيل (`ismail`)
- **Ayah:** 37:100–101. **Engine:** the living sky from dusk through night to dawn; an infant of light.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* Years passed in the land where they had settled, and Sarah had no children. In Egypt she had been given a companion, Hajar, and she gave Hajar to Ibrahim as a wife. (2) *Say:* Old by now, Ibrahim prayed for a son who would be righteous. (3) **Verse 37:100-101**, placed sky.
- **Sound:** sheep, air → crickets, fire → birds, air. **Art:** Sarah and Hajar appear only as dim figures of light; Ismail, newborn, as a small light. Ibrahim appears as a steady light. That Hajar came to Sarah in Egypt and that Sarah gave her to Ibrahim is told in the hadith (al-Bukhari). The camp in the land of Canaan is imagined.
- **Image prompt:** A camp of dark goat-hair tents among olive trees and green hills at dusk, two dim amber figures of light by the tents and a steady white light before them; then night, the white light kneeling in prayer under a great field of stars and a rising moon; then dawn, and beside the second tent a tiny new light wrapped in white; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 70 · The Barren Valley · الوادي القاحل (`valley`)
- **Ayah:** 14:37. **Engine:** heat shimmer over a barren valley; a walk into the distance.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* At God's command, Ibrahim brought Hajar and the infant Ismail to a dry valley among bare mountains, where no one lived. (2) *Say:* He left them dates and a skin of water, and turned to go. Hajar asked: did God command you this? Yes, he said. Then He will not abandon us, she answered. (3) **Verse 14:37**, placed sky.
- **Sound:** desert, wind → desert → wind. **Art:** The valley is the place that became Makkah; its look is imagined. Hajar appears as a dim figure of light, the infant Ismail as a small light, Ibrahim as a steady light, far away and from behind. Hajar's question and Ibrahim's prayer at the pass are told in the hadith (al-Bukhari).
- **Image prompt:** A vast barren valley between dark jagged mountains under a white noon sun, heat shimmering over pale sand, a single thorny tree; a dim amber figure carrying a tiny light walking beside a steady white light; then the white light walking away toward a high pass while the amber figure waits by the tree; then from the pass, the white light seen from behind with a hand raised, looking down at the two tiny lights far below; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 71 · The Spring · زمزم (`zamzam`)
- **Ayah:** 2:158 (its opening words). **Engine:** the spring welling in rings; seven runs counted in numerals; birds circling.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* When the water ran out, the child grew restless with thirst. Hajar climbed the nearest hill, Safa, to look for anyone who might help. (2) *Say:* Seeing no one, she ran across the valley to the hill of Marwa, and back: seven times. (3) *Say:* Then she heard a sound. An angel stood where the child lay, and water welled up from the ground. (4) **Verse 2:158** (its opening words, on as-Safa and al-Marwah), placed sky.
- **Sound:** desert, wind → desert → spring, air → spring, birds. **Art:** Hajar's search between the hills, the angel and the spring are told in the hadith (al-Bukhari). The angel appears only as radiance; Hajar as a dim figure of light; the child only as a small light. The two hills are Safa and Marwa, between which pilgrims still walk; the spring is Zamzam. Their look here is imagined.
- **Image prompt:** A barren valley at noon between two low rocky hills, a dim amber figure of light running back and forth along the valley floor between them, numerals one to seven glowing and fading in the air; a tiny light lying under a thorn tree; a tall figure of pure white radiance beside it, and clear water welling up from the sand in widening rings, glittering; birds of light circling above; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 72 · The Dream · رؤيا إبراهيم (`dream`)
- **Ayah:** 37:102. **Engine:** the living sky through a night; stars turning.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* Ibrahim came often to the valley, where a tribe had settled by the spring and Ismail had grown into a young man. (2) *Say:* Then he saw in a dream that he was sacrificing his son; and the dreams of prophets are true. (3) *Say:* In the morning he told Ismail what he had seen, and asked him what he thought. (4) **Verse 37:102**, placed sky.
- **Sound:** desert, voices → crickets, air → wind, air → wind. **Art:** Ibrahim and Ismail appear only as steady lights, far away. The dream is shown only as the night sky. - **Image prompt:** A small camp of tents beside a stone-rimmed spring in a mountain valley at sunset, a tall steady white light arriving and a slightly smaller steady light coming to meet him; then night, the elder light lying still beneath a sky of slowly turning stars; then at dawn the two lights walking together toward the hills; amber, indigo, rose; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 73 · The Ransom · الفداء (`ransom`)
- **Ayah:** 37:104–107. **Engine:** a coil of smoke recoiling; a son bowed in prostration; a ram of light at sunrise.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* On the way, Satan came three times to turn them back; each time Ibrahim drove him off with stones, the stones pilgrims still throw. (2) *Say:* Both gave themselves to God's command, and Ibrahim laid his son down on his forehead. (3) *Say:* Then a call came, and in the boy's place stood a great ram. (4) **Verse 37:104-107**, placed sky.
- **Sound:** wind, air → air → wind, birds → air, birds. **Art:** Satan appears only as a coil of smoke, and the stones are told rather than shown: the account of Ibn Abbas (in the Musnad of Ahmad). No blade is shown and no one is harmed. Ibrahim and Ismail appear only as steady lights, far away; the ram appears only as light. The place is imagined after the valley of Mina.
- **Image prompt:** A narrow rocky valley at first light, two steady lights climbing a winding path, a coil of dark smoke curling toward them and recoiling; then on a hillside at sunrise the smaller light bowed low to the ground and the taller kneeling beside it; then a great ram made of soft white light standing beside them as the sun rises; rose, gold, slate; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 76 · Raising the House · بناء البيت (`house`)
- **Ayah:** 2:127. **Engine:** walls raised course by course; the Station rising with them.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* Years later Ibrahim came again and told Ismail that God had commanded him to build a House there, and asked for his help. (2) *Say:* Ismail brought the stones, and Ibrahim set them, course upon course. (3) *Say:* When the walls grew high, Ibrahim stood on a stone to reach them: the stone still kept beside the House, called the Station of Ibrahim. (4) **Verse 2:127**, placed sky.

- **Image prompt:** A valley among dark mountains, a few tents and a stone-rimmed spring; two steady lights raising walls of dark blue-grey stone course by course, the younger carrying stones from the hillside, the elder standing on a single stone that lifts him as the walls grow; at evening the finished walls of the House stand plain and square, the two lights before it with hands raised; ochre, slate, gold; ink-wash and parchment texture.

## What the engine shows and what it approximates

- Sky time-lapses compress hours into seconds; bodies move along simple arcs, not true orbits.
- Shards are cut from the painted idol; they tumble without colliding.
- Flames are painted noise, not a fluid simulation; the cool fire is a colour and motion change.
- The House is painted on one plane (its side face drawn in perspective), so the camera keeps near the
  front; the pilgrims circle it in two halves, behind and in front of that plane.
- The birds' and the son's tests are told, never shown: the birds fly to the hills and come back, the son
  bows, a ram appears; no blade, no harm.
- Hajar's seven runs are compressed into one beat, counted in numerals.
- The verse beats wait for their ayat (`pendingVerse`) until api.alquran.cloud can be reached.
