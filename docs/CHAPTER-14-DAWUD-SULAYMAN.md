# Chapter 14 — Dawud and Sulayman (داود وسليمان)

Dhul-Kifl, who kept his word; Talut's army tested at the river; young Dawud bringing down Jalut; the
mountains and birds joining Dawud's praise; iron made soft in his hands; Sulayman's wiser judgment of the
trampled vineyard; his kingdom of men, jinn and birds; the ant's warning; the hoopoe's news of Saba; the
queen on the floor of glass; and Sulayman's death, leaning on his staff, unknown to the jinn.

**Content rules kept throughout.** God is never depicted. The prophets appear only as steady lights;
Talut, Jalut, the armies, the queen and the people as dim lights; the jinn as smouldering shadows. Jalut's
fall and Sulayman's death are lights going out. Dawud's recitation is a wordless voice, never music.

**Verses.** 21:85 is shown in full. Every other verse beat waits as `pendingVerse` (placed sky) until
api.alquran.cloud can be reached; several are parts of ayat chosen by hand once fetched.

### 149 · Dhul-Kifl · ذو الكفل (`kifl`)
- **Ayah:** 21:85. **Node:** dhulkifl.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* Among the prophets the Quran names is Dhul-Kifl, a man known for his patience. (2) *Say:* It is said he promised to fast every day, to pray every night, and to judge between people without ever losing his temper; and he kept his word. (3) **Verse 21:85**, placed sky.
- **Sound:** voices, wind → wind, voices → crickets, air. **Art:** The prophets appear only as steady lights; kings, armies, the queen and the people as dim figures of light; the jinn as smouldering shadows. The look of the land is imagined. Who Dhul-Kifl was is debated among scholars; that he pledged to fast by day, pray by night and judge without anger is from the commentaries.
- **Image prompt:** A town gate of pale stone at dusk, a steady light seated beneath its arch with dim lights of people waiting to be heard; then days and nights racing past in the sky while the light stays at his place, kneeling in prayer by night; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 150 · The Chest of Tranquillity · التابوت (`tabut`)
- **Ayah:** 2:248. **Node:** dawud.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* Long after Musa, the Children of Israel asked their prophet for a king to lead them in God's cause, and God chose Talut. (2) *Say:* God chose him and gave him knowledge and strength, their prophet said, and here is the sign: the chest will come to you, carried by angels, bringing tranquillity from your Lord and what was left by the families of Musa and Harun. - **Sound:** crowd, jeer → crowd, air → air. **Art:** The prophet of the Children of Israel in Talut's time is named Shamwil (Samuel) in the commentaries; he and the people appear only as lights. The angels who carried the chest are columns of light (2:248). The chest's relics are not shown.
- **Image prompt:** A hill town in the land of Israel at morning, crowds of dim lights gathered before a steady light, murmuring against a tall plain figure of light chosen as king; then columns of soft golden light coming over the hill bearing a gilded chest on poles, and setting it down before the people; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 155 · The Two Disputants · المتخاصمان (`disputants`)
- **Ayah:** 38:24. **Node:** dawud.
- **Beats.** (1) *Say:* One night, while Dawud was praying alone, two men climbed over the wall of his chamber. Do not be afraid, they said; we are two in a dispute. Judge between us with truth. (2) *Say:* This brother of mine has ninety-nine ewes, and I have one; yet he says, give it to me, and overpowers me with words. Dawud said: he has wronged you by asking for your ewe. (3) *Say:* Then Dawud understood that he was the one being tested. He asked his Lord's forgiveness, fell down bowing, and turned to Him in repentance. (4) **Verse 38:24** (from "And David became certain that We had tried him"), placed sky.
- **Sound:** air, echo → sheep, wind → air → air. **Art:** Dawud and the two men appear only as lights. Who the two were is not told in the Quran; many commentators say they were angels sent to test him. The flock is drawn as the case they described.
- **Image prompt:** Dawud's prayer chamber at night, a steady light at prayer; two dim figures of light appearing on top of the high wall and dropping inside; then a vision of their case: a great flock of ninety-nine ewes on one side and a single ewe alone on the other; then the steady light bowed low in repentance; ink-wash and parchment texture.
