# Chapter 5 — Prophet Salih (صالح)

The brief for the chapter after Hud, written in the same form as the chapters before it: scenes with
their visuals, camera, sound, narration and the ayah shown on screen. The scene files in
`src/story/chapters/c5/` are built from it. Image prompts follow the shared Troy + Three Kingdoms style
(`npm run prompts` adds the style and the content rules to each one in `docs/IMAGE-PROMPTS.md`).

**Content rules kept throughout.** God is never depicted, in any form or light. Salih appears only as a
figure of steady white-gold light, far away or from behind; the people of Thamud as figures of dim amber
light; no faces, ever. The she-camel, a sign from God, is a creature of soft light. Nothing violent is
shown: the she-camel's light and the lights of Thamud simply go out. No music; sound beds are wind, rock,
water, crowds and voices without words. Narration says "God". Quran text is never typed: every ayah comes
from `src/data/ayahs.json` by reference, Arabic and Sahih International English.

**Setting.** The valley of al-Hijr in north-west Arabia, where tradition places Thamud: red and gold
sandstone outcrops standing alone in a sandy plain, homes and tombs carved straight into them, springs,
palm groves and fields on the valley floor.

## Scenes

### 50 · The Valley of the Rock · وادي الحجر (`hijr`)
- **Ayah:** 89:9 (whole), following 89:6–8 that opened Chapter 4.
- **Beats.**
  1. Out of a high, hazy dawn the camera sinks toward a plain of red rock towers.
     *Say:* Generations after ʿAd, another people rose in the north, in a valley of red rock: Thamud.
  2. Gliding low between the outcrops, carved facades paint in, one rock after another.
     *Say:* They cut their homes straight into the mountains, as no one had done before them.
  3. A high facade, half carved: figures of dim amber light on its ledges, dust falling from their work.
     *Say:* Their palaces stood on the plains, and their homes in the rock were made to last forever.
  4. The whole valley at full light. **Verse 89:9.**
- **Sound:** wind, echo → stone, work → desert.
- **Art notes:** the carved homes follow the tombs of al-Hijr (Hegra), traditionally identified with
  Thamud; their people appear only as dim lights.
- **Image prompt:** A long descent out of a pale dawn sky over a wide sandy plain studded with enormous
  free-standing outcrops of red and gold sandstone, wind-carved into domes, towers and folds; the camera
  glides low between them as monumental facades appear carved straight into the rock faces, crowned with
  stepped battlements, pilasters framing tall dark doorways; on the highest facade tiny figures of dim
  amber light stand on narrow ledges, chips of stone and dust drifting down in the low sun; long violet
  shadows across the sand, a thin haze over the far rocks; rose red, ochre, gold and deep violet; ink-wash
  and parchment texture.

### 51 · Gardens and Springs · خيرات الوادي (`plenty`)
- **Ayah:** 26:146–149 (Salih's words to them about their gardens, springs, palms and carved homes).
- **Beats.**
  1. The valley floor: springs running between palm groves, fields of grain.
     *Say:* The valley floor was green: springs ran between the palms, and the fields were heavy with grain.
  2. Herds of camels moving to water across the plain, the rocks behind.
     *Say:* Their herds grew, and they lived secure, sure that it would always be so.
  3. Evening: lamps glow in the carved doorways. **Verse 26:146–149.**
- **Sound:** spring, birds, rustle → herd, desert → crickets, city.
- **Art notes:** the groves, fields and herds are imagined from the verses.
- **Image prompt:** The floor of a valley of red sandstone towers turned green: clear springs running
  between groves of date palms heavy with soft golden fruit, small fields of ripe grain in irrigated
  squares, a long line of camels walking to water with their shadows beside them; behind, the great carved
  rock facades glowing rose and gold in the evening sun, warm lamplight beginning to show in their dark
  doorways; abundance and calm; emerald, gold, rose red and lamp amber; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 52 · Salih Among His People · صالح بين قومه (`salih`)
- **Ayah:** 11:61 (the call to worship God alone, who produced them from the earth and settled them in it).
- **Beats.**
  1. Wide: a single steady light walks into the city from the desert road, seen from far behind.
     *Say:* Among them lived one of their own, Salih, trusted and wise, and his light was not like theirs.
  2. He stands on a low rock in the square; dim lights gather around; soft light spreads from him.
     *Say:* He called them to worship God alone, who had brought them out of the earth and settled them in it.
  3. **Verse 11:61.**
- **Sound:** city, desert → crowd, voice → voice, air.
- **Art notes:** Salih is shown only as a figure of steady white-gold light, from far away or from behind.
- **Image prompt:** A city of homes carved into towering red rock outcrops, a wide sandy square between
  them crowded with figures of dim amber light; walking in from the desert road, small and far away and seen
  from behind, a single figure of steady white-gold light; then the same figure standing on a low flat rock
  at the centre of the square, a soft wave of light spreading from it over the crowd, white-gold Arabic
  calligraphy forming in the air above; warm afternoon light, rose and ochre stone, dust in the air;
  ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 53 · Bring Us a Sign · تحدي القوم (`doubt`)
- **Ayah:** 26:153–154 (they call him bewitched, a man like them, and demand a sign).
- **Beats.**
  1. The crowd turns on him: jagged strokes of scorn swirl around the steady light.
     *Say:* Their chiefs answered that he had been their hope, and now he spoke against the ways of their fathers.
  2. Larger, darker amber figures on a carved terrace above the square; the great rock behind them.
     *Say:* Bring us a sign, they said, if you are telling the truth.
  3. **Verse 26:153–154.**
- **Sound:** crowd, jeer → jeer, voice → air.
- **Art notes:** the crowd's scorn is drawn as jagged strokes, never words; the chiefs are dim figures.
- **Image prompt:** The crowded square of the rock city at golden hour, hundreds of figures of dim amber
  light pressing toward a single small steady white light, jagged dark ink strokes of scorn swirling around
  it without ever touching it; above, on a carved terrace, a few larger, darker amber figures stand with
  arms raised toward an enormous lone outcrop of red rock behind the city; tension, heat and dust; burnt
  orange, rose stone, ink black; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 54 · The She-Camel · الناقة (`camel`)
- **Ayah:** 7:73 (the she-camel of God, a sign: let her eat on God's earth and do not touch her with harm).
- **Beats.**
  1. Before the great lone rock, the people gather as dim lights; Salih's light stands before it.
     *Say:* They gathered before a great rock and waited, sure that nothing would happen.
  2. Lines of light open in the rock; a she-camel of soft light steps out onto the sand.
     *Say:* Then, as they watched, a she-camel came out to them: a sign that no one could deny.
  3. **Verse 7:73.**
- **Sound:** crowd, air → rumble, swell, echo → air.
- **Art notes:** that she came out of the rock is from the commentaries (tradition), not from the Quran's
  words; she is drawn as a creature of soft light.
- **Image prompt:** At dawn, an enormous lone outcrop of red sandstone at the edge of the city, a great
  crowd of dim amber lights gathered before it at a distance, one small steady white light standing nearest
  the rock; thin lines of white-gold light opening across the rock face, light spilling from within, and a
  tall she-camel made of soft luminous light stepping out onto the sand, calm and unhurried; the crowd's
  lights drawing back; violet dawn shadows, rose stone, pearl and gold light; reverent, quiet, immense;
  ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 55 · The Water Shared · قسمة الماء (`drink`)
- **Ayah:** 26:155–156 (a day to drink for her and a day for them; do not touch her with harm).
- **Beats.**
  1. The great well of the valley: the she-camel drinks while the people keep back.
     *Say:* The water of the valley was shared: one day the she-camel drank, and the next day the people.
  2. Days and nights flicker past: she comes and goes over the plain; herds come on their days.
     *Say:* She grazed freely on God's earth, and for a time they let her be.
  3. **Verse 26:155–156.**
- **Sound:** water, desert → herd, air → water.
- **Art notes:** the well and the alternating days are imagined from the verses.
- **Image prompt:** A great round stone well on the sandy floor of the rock valley, its curb worn smooth,
  water glinting deep inside; the she-camel of soft light drinking alone at a stone trough beside it while
  the people's dim amber lights wait far back among the palms; then a time-lapse of days and nights passing
  over the valley, the she-camel's light crossing the plain to graze and the herds coming to the well on
  their own days; rose stone, cool water blue, pale gold; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 56 · The Night of the Nine · ليلة التسعة (`nine`)
- **Ayah:** 91:11–14 (Thamud denied; the most wretched went forth; the messenger's warning; they hamstrung her).
- **Beats.**
  1. Night in a carved hall: nine figures like dark embers lean together; jagged strokes pass between them.
     *Say:* In the city were nine men who spread corruption and would not set things right.
  2. One dark ember goes out alone across the moonlit plain toward the she-camel's light.
     *Say:* They grew tired of the sign and of the one who brought it, and the most wretched of them went out against her.
  3. Her light goes out; only the moon and the rocks remain.
     *Say:* By morning her light was gone from the valley.
  4. **Verse 91:11–14.**
- **Sound:** crickets, voices → air, crickets → wind → air.
- **Art notes:** the nine men (27:48) and the one who went out (91:12) appear only as dark embers; the
  act is never shown: her light simply goes out.
- **Image prompt:** Night in the rock valley under a cold moon: inside a carved hall lit by one small fire,
  nine dim figures like dark embers leaning together, faint jagged strokes passing between them; then a
  single dark ember crossing the silver plain alone toward a soft light resting beside the well; then the
  plain empty, the well and the towering rocks in moonlight, the soft light gone; deep indigo, silver,
  ember red; restrained and grave, nothing violent shown; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 57 · Three Days · المهلة (`three`)
- **Ayah:** 11:65 (enjoy yourselves in your homes for three days; a promise not to be denied).
- **Beats.**
  1. From behind, Salih's light on a rise above the city, facing it.
     *Say:* Salih told them to enjoy their homes for three days, for then it would come.
  2. Days and nights flicker over the valley; numerals count three days; the city's lamps burn on.
     *Say:* For three days and nights they waited in their houses of stone, still mocking.
  3. On the last night, Salih and those who believed walk out of the valley.
     *Say:* On the last night, Salih and those who believed left the valley.
  4. **Verse 11:65.**
- **Sound:** wind, air → wind, crickets → desert, crickets → air.
- **Art notes:** the numerals counting the days come from the verse's "three days"; the departure is
  imagined from 11:66.
- **Image prompt:** A small steady white light seen from behind on a rocky rise above the valley of carved
  rock homes, facing them in silence; then days and nights flickering past over the valley, pale Arabic
  numerals one, two, three appearing and fading in the sky, the lamps in the carved doorways still burning;
  then, by starlight, a short line of steady white lights walking out of the valley into the open desert;
  violet dusk, indigo night, rose stone; ink-wash and parchment texture.

### 58 · The Blast · الفجر الأخير (`blast`)
- **Ayah:** 11:67–68 (the blast seized the wrongdoers; as if they had never prospered there).
- **Beats.**
  1. The fourth dawn: the valley still, lamps burning in every doorway.
     *Say:* On the fourth morning a single, terrible blast tore through the valley, and the earth shook.
  2. A white flash and a wave of dust; every lamp in every carved doorway goes out.
     *Say:* In every carved home, every light went out, and the valley fell silent.
  3. **Verse 11:67–68.**
- **Sound:** air, desert → boom with thunder and rumble → echo, air.
- **Art notes:** no one is shown suffering: the punishment is shown through the rock, the dust and the
  light alone, and the lights simply go out.
- **Image prompt:** The fourth dawn over the valley of carved rock homes, every doorway still glowing with
  lamplight; then a single blinding white flash and a wall of dust sweeping across the plain, the great
  rocks shuddering; then, as the dust settles, every doorway dark and the valley utterly silent under a grey
  sky; nothing violent shown, only light going out; ash grey, bone white, cold rose stone; ink-wash and
  parchment texture.

### 59 · Salih Turns Away · وداع صالح (`farewell`)
- **Ayah:** 7:79 (he turned away: I conveyed my Lord's message and advised you, but you do not like advisors).
- **Beats.**
  1. Far off on a ridge, from behind, Salih's light looks back at the silent valley.
     *Say:* Salih looked back once at the silent valley he had tried to save.
  2. He turns and walks on with the believers into the desert.
     *Say:* Then he turned away, grieving, and went on with those who believed.
  3. Centuries pass: the carved homes stand empty in the sand.
     *Say:* Their houses stand empty in the rock to this day, a sign for those who know.
  4. **Verse 7:79.**
- **Sound:** wind, air → desert → wind, echo → air.
- **Art notes:** Salih and the believers appear only as steady lights, far away; the empty homes today
  follow the tombs of al-Hijr; the closing words echo 27:52.
- **Image prompt:** A single steady white light seen from far behind on a high ridge of red rock, looking
  down over a silent valley of empty carved homes; then a short line of white lights walking away into an
  endless desert; then centuries passing in a breath, sand drifting against the carved facades, their
  doorways dark and empty under a vast quiet sky; elegiac and still; rose stone, pale sand, faded gold,
  dusk blue; ink-wash and parchment texture.

## What the engine shows and what it approximates

- The she-camel is a painted card of light: she glides rather than walks, and her drinking is a pose.
- The rock opening is a glow spreading along painted fissures, not a breaking rock.
- The blast is a flash, a wave of dust and the camera shaking; the lamps go out as a crossfade.
- The days pass as the world's day and night cycle with numerals, as in Chapter 4's wind.
